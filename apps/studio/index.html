Simulation

Hold Ctrl and drag a bone to pull the body about; the camera holds still while you do. At 1× a hold carries a good fraction of the body's weight.

Simulated time produced per second of real time. Set it to the profile's own rate for life speed, lower for slow motion with every tick still taken. A fixed step never skips a tick — what the default drops when it falls behind is elapsed real time — and this does not discard either: ask for more than the machine can sustain and it falls behind rather than skipping. Pause and use Step to go a tick at a time.

A hundred and eight units: seven at each elbow (both heads of biceps, brachialis, brachioradialis, all three heads of triceps), eight at each shoulder (the three heads of deltoid, the rotator cuff, teres major), ten at each knee (the four of the quadriceps, both heads of biceps femoris, semitendinosus, semimembranosus and both heads of gastrocnemius) and twenty at each hip (two parts of gluteus maximus, three each of medius and minimus, iliacus and psoas, the three adductors in six parts, piriformis, tensor fasciae latae, gracilis and sartorius) and nine at each ankle (soleus, both tibialis, both fibularis, and the four long toe muscles, which stop at the metatarsals because the dataset marks no phalanx). Peak force comes from the MyoSuite arm and leg models; attachments come from each side's own markers, and both lengths are derived against this skeleton — the tendon fitted to the path it has here, the fibre scaled by how far the muscle travels here against how far it travels there. Each slider drives its own muscles on both sides at once, and is squared, so the readout shows the excitation given rather than where the handle sits.
